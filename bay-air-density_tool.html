Why speed counts twice

Speed appears twice in the chain. It sets how much air arrives each second (step 1) and how much momentum each kilogram carries (step 3). Density appears once (step 2). So the push grows with speed squared: in your air a flat sail feels 20.5 N/m² at 10 kn, 82.1 N/m² at 20 kn and 328 N/m² at 40 kn.

At 15 kn, swapping hot, saturated air (90°F) for cold, dry air (40°F) adds as much push as 0.9 kn more wind.

Evaporating fog

Fog is liquid water hanging in the air. When fog drifts into drier air, the droplets evaporate and the heat for that comes out of the air, so the air cools.10 Evaporation absorbs energy. Condensation, when fog forms, gives it back.

0.20 g/m³

Liquid water content of the fog, in grams per cubic meter of air.

Published values8

Evaporating 0.20 g/m³ takes 492 J of heat out of each cubic meter of air, about 14 times the wind’s kinetic energy at 15 kn. That heat goes into the water vapor, not into the wind. The only effect at your sail is the +0.11% change in density from the cooler air.

The droplets evaporate into the air set above, with no outside heat, as in the wet-bulb process.9 When sunshine burns fog off, the sun supplies the heat instead.

Moist air is lighter

A water vapor molecule (18.015 g/mol) is lighter than the average dry-air molecule (28.965 g/mol) it replaces. At 62°F, 75% humidity makes the air 0.53% lighter than dry air. Dry air would have to be 2.8°F warmer to be this light (the virtual temperature).313

Warm air holds more water

Air at 90°F can hold 34.2 g/m³ of water vapor. At 40°F it holds only 6.6 g/m³, about a fifth as much. So going from dry to saturated thins the air 1.8% at 90°F and only 0.31% at 40°F.2

Temperature matters most

From 40°F to 90°F at 75% humidity, the air thins 10.1%. Humidity alone never moves it more than 1.8% in this range. Cold, dry air pushes 12% harder than hot, saturated air at the same wind speed.

How this is worked out

  • Density. Dry air plus water vapor, each from the ideal gas law, with molar masses from CIPM-2007.3 It reads 0.03–0.05% below the full CIPM-2007 formula, which adds small real-gas corrections.
  • Water vapor. Saturation pressure from the IAPWS equation of Wagner and Pruß, with Buck (1996) for dew points below 32°F.211
  • Momentum and push. Force is momentum delivered per second.7 Wind pressure is ½ρv²,5 and a flat plate facing the wind has a force coefficient of 1.28.6
  • Fog. Droplets evaporate at constant pressure with no outside heat,9 using the moist-air enthalpy balance: 1.006 kJ/(kg·K) for dry air, 1.86 kJ/(kg·K) for vapor and 2501 kJ/kg to evaporate water at 32°F.12
  • Not included. Gusts, wind chill, how steady the breeze is, and sail shapes other than a flat plate.
