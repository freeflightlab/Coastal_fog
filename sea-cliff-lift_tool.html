Lapse rates: does rising air keep rising?

Air pushed up the cliff cools as it rises: about 5.35°F per 1,000 ft while it is clear,1432 and roughly half that once it is in fog, because condensing water gives its heat to the air.1532 When your air cools faster than the saturated rate but slower than the dry rate, it is conditionally unstable: rising air that saturates can end up lighter than its surroundings and get an extra push upward.272829 That only works if the air around it is drier, so humidity at cliff-top height matters. If the rising air ends up heavier, the wind still forces it up the face, but it stops rising soon after it clears the top.

Fog forming and evaporating

Condensing fog gives heat to the air around it, and evaporating fog takes the same heat back.10 Fog that forms as air rides up the cliff helps lift. Fog that evaporates into drier air chills it and makes sink.

Forming as air rides up the cliff

Air riding up the cliff reaches its dew point at 200 ft, so fog forms on the face there. Between there and the top, 0.25 g of water condenses out of each kilogram of air and releases 627 J/kg of heat. That keeps the rising air 1.07°F warmer than clear air would be, worth +0.24 m/s of lift here, after the weight of the droplets.

Sun burning it off

Evaporating a fog layer 1,200 ft deep holding 0.20 g/m³ takes 180 kJ per square meter of ground. Full sunshine, about 1,000 W/m², supplies that in 3.0 minutes at the very least. Burn-off is slower in practice, because only part of the sunlight goes into evaporating droplets. Sunlight pays that heat, so fog burned off by the sun does not chill the air.

Sun and heat transfer

Sunshine heats rock and sand, and they heat the air touching them. At 10.0 kn into the cliff, the air riding up the face moves past so fast that 150 W/m² warms it by only +0.043°F on average. So in ridge-lift wind the sun mostly works in other ways. It builds pockets of warm air that break away as thermals, typically about 1.2 m/s here.22 It also heats the land faster than the sea, which drives the onshore sea breeze.25 In lighter wind the same heating warms the air more, and thermals matter more.

Air density reference

What sets the density at the beach, from 40°F to 90°F.

Moist air is lighter

A water vapor molecule (18.015 g/mol) is lighter than the average dry-air molecule (28.965 g/mol) it replaces. At 60°F, 97% humidity makes the air 0.64% lighter than dry air. Dry air would have to be 3.3°F warmer to be this light (the virtual temperature).313

Warm air holds more water

Air at 90°F can hold 34.2 g/m³ of water vapor. At 40°F it holds only 6.6 g/m³, about a fifth as much. So going from dry to saturated thins the air 1.8% at 90°F and only 0.31% at 40°F.2

Temperature matters most

From 40°F to 90°F at 97% humidity, the air thins 10.4%. Humidity alone never moves it more than 1.8% in this range. Cold, dry air pushes 12% harder than hot, saturated air at the same wind speed.

How this is worked out

  • Density. Dry air plus water vapor from the ideal gas law, with CIPM-2007 molar masses,3 and saturation vapor pressure from the IAPWS equation of Wagner and Pruß.211
  • Ridge lift. Wind into the cliff × the sine of the airflow’s slope angle, matching the FAA example and the slope-times-wind method used in soaring forecasts.1819
  • Rising air. A parcel of beach air lifted up the face cools at the adiabatic rate for moist clear air (the dry rate, about 1% slower because of its water vapor) until it saturates, then at the saturated rate.141512 The surrounding air follows your lapse rate, with humidity changing evenly from the beach value to the cliff-top value. Buoyancy compares densities, so it counts temperature, water vapor and the weight of fog droplets.13 The budget starts from temperature alone. Humidity, fog and sun can each unlock the others, so the boost they make together is shared between them by averaging over every order they could be added in (Shapley values).
  • Upper limits. Buoyancy gains use parcel theory with no mixing.20 Real rising air mixes with the air around it, which dilutes buoyancy, and climate-model studies find that stronger mixing reduces condensation heating.3031 When rising air ends up heavier than its surroundings, the wind still forces it up the face; the deficit only stops it rising above the top. Clear rising air can be lighter than its surroundings only when the surrounding air, counting humidity, is unstable on its own, and the page flags those setups.
  • Stability. The Froude number U/(NH) uses the wind into the cliff, the cliff height and the stability of air riding up it.21 Below 1, air under the dividing-streamline height H(1 − Fr) flows around the cliff, so ridge lift is scaled by Fr.26 Above the cliff top, rising air coasts until stable air stops it.
  • Sun. Average warming of the air riding up the face is the heat flux divided by air density × specific heat × wind into the cliff.12 Thermal strength is the Deardorff velocity.22
  • Your wing. Lift and drag scale with density × speed², so true airspeed and sink both scale with √(1.225 ÷ density).5 Density altitude is the standard-atmosphere height with the same density.123
  • Not included. Turbulence and rotor, wind changing with height, the wing’s full speed polar, and the cliff’s three-dimensional shape.
